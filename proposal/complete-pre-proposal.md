# 1. Executive Summary

Marindustrial Inc. has an opportunity to improve operational efficiency through internally developed software tailored to existing business processes. Warehouse, inventory, reporting, shipping and administrative work can involve repeated entry, document handling and switching between applications. A dedicated internal software-development capability could complement SAP Business One 10.0, reduce unnecessary work and support improvements across Mississauga, Montreal, Dartmouth and Moncton.

Five initial pilots illustrate this potential: **Automated Inventory Cycle Counting; Centralized Reporting & Automated Business Intelligence; Automated Batch Shipping & SAP Integration; Warehouse Slotting & Picking Optimization; and RF Scanner & Warehouse Management System.** These projects are examples of what an ongoing Software Developer could identify, build, maintain and improve—not a one-time list of five applications. Direct-Ship Processing & SAP Invoice Management has moved to the near-term future backlog.

Preliminary research suggests that several processes warrant measured testing. Annual physical inventory counts have involved about **four employees for two to three working days** and can interrupt normal operations. Shipping administration currently takes approximately **75 seconds per shipment** in the observed workflow versus a **proposed**, unvalidated 10–15-second assisted target. A reported 12-month shipment total of **2,020 completed shipments** implies a scenario of **33.7–36.5 hours of handling time released per year if every shipment were eligible and the target achieved**—not a confirmed saving. A weekly service-report process is preliminarily estimated at 1–2 hours of manual work. Actual pick frequency, travel savings, RF productivity and recurring maintenance costs are not yet established.

The immediate recommendation is controlled pilot testing: document baseline work, develop limited solutions, verify correctness and security, and compare end-to-end processes—including exceptions, employee review and operating cost. Recovered time is employee capacity, not necessarily a cash or payroll reduction. Larger investments should depend on evidence rather than hypothetical ROI.

**Management approval requested:** authorize a supervised, limited-scope evaluation with defined process owners, reasonable test access, measurable success criteria and separate approval for spending. If trials demonstrate repeatable, sustainable value, Marindustrial could consider a dedicated internal **Software Developer** role to integrate and maintain successful applications and pursue further opportunities across departments.

# 4. Estimated Cost-Benefit Summary

The five pilots address distinct challenges: accuracy, operational continuity, repeated administration, walking distance and warehouse throughput. Available working evidence does **not** support an additive cash-savings or ROI forecast. Employee time released is not automatically a reduction in payroll.

| Pilot | Available baseline and scenario | Expected value / open validation |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Cycle counting** | Annual physical count: about 4 employees for 2–3 eight-hour days (**64–96 direct person-hours**). Provisional ABC cadence: **30/90/365 calendar days** | Potentially less shutdown and earlier variance detection; **net labour savings not established**. Count coverage, physical item mix, exception labour and audit requirements must be tested. |
| **2. Reporting/BI** | One weekly service report estimated at **1–2 hours/week**, or **52–104 existing workload hours/year** | Net time saved is unknown until complete reporting, review, exceptions and maintenance are timed. |
| **3. Batch shipping/SAP** | **75 seconds/shipment** current; **10–15 seconds** assisted **target**. Historical review: **2,020 completed shipments** Oct 2025–Sep 2026 | If all were eligible and achieved target: **33.7–36.5 projected annual handling hours**, based on 60–65 seconds removed per shipment (**80.0–86.7%**). Actual eligibility, reliability and costs remain unverified. |
| **4. Warehouse slotting** | Five years of SAP records and bin data are candidate inputs; actual local pick frequency and routes must be validated | Possible reduced walking and full pick-to-stage time; **no verified percentage or annual savings**. Deduct relocation and disruption costs. |
| **5. RF Scanner/WMS** | An existing codebase is a development starting point; feature readiness needs verification | Potential accuracy and productivity gains only for distinct workflows, excluding time already credited to Projects 1, 3 or 4. |

**Shipping arithmetic:** 2,020 shipments × (60–65 seconds) ÷ 3,600 = **33.7–36.5 hours**. This remains a hypothetical full-eligibility scenario, not realized savings. **Reporting arithmetic:** 1–2 hours/week × 52 = **52–104 hours of current workload**, not proven savings. Do not add these different measures and describe the sum as achieved benefit.

Evaluation must include development, data access, SAP/carrier interfaces, licences, hosting, devices, relocation, security, testing, training and ongoing maintenance. Present time released, avoided expense, continuity and error-rate changes separately. A small automation may have modest direct dollar savings yet usefully demonstrate the ability to improve many repeated workflows; no speculative company-wide multiplier is claimed.

# 5. Pilot Approach & Management Request

## Proposed Pilot Approach

Marindustrial can begin with a **proposed 60-day, supervised pilot evaluation** of one or more selected workflows. The duration is a working proposal, not an authorized deadline. Management and the responsible employees would agree on scope, measurable success criteria, source data, an operational owner and acceptable access before any integration or live process changes.

Initial development should use representative transactions and approved read-only access or a SAP test environment where practical. Pilot work can be phased rather than launching five applications at once. Correctness, permissions, exception handling, rollback and employee acceptance take priority over rapid automation. Any material paid services, SAP posting capabilities, relocation of inventory or customer-facing action must receive separate authorization.

## Evaluation & Success Measures

For every selected workflow, measure the complete **manual versus software-assisted process**, representative annual volume, corrections, exceptions, employee review, data quality and operating impact. Annualize only evidence-backed improvements and separately record build time, licences, integrations, devices, relocation, hosting, training and maintenance. Do not treat projected targets as achieved results.

Cycle counting requires Finance/audit review before reducing the annual physical count. Slotting should simulate and then carefully trial current-versus-proposed placements with real physical-pick events, accurate bin distances, safety constraints and relocation costs. Shipping requires correct labels, tracking and SAP documents. RF and slotting benefits must not credit the same walking reduction twice.

At the proposed evaluation's conclusion, summarize measured outcomes, risks, cost of upkeep and a recommendation to continue, revise, pause or stop each candidate.

## Management Support Requested

- Assign a sponsor, relevant process owners and a limited scope with acceptance criteria.
- Allow reasonable observation, development, testing and user-review time.
- Approve necessary system/data access through management, IT and SAP/carrier providers.
- Require safe test data, reconciliations, audit logging, user training and rollback plans.
- Approve new integration fees, equipment or hosting **separately** before expense is incurred.

## Longer-Term Opportunity

Approval now is for **pilot evaluation**, not for all five deployments or an automatic permanent position. If the work demonstrates sustainable, repeatable operational value, management can assess a dedicated internal **Software Developer** role to design, deploy, integrate and maintain improvements throughout Marindustrial.

# Project 1 — Automated Inventory Cycle Counting

## Current Challenge

Marindustrial's annual inventory count has historically required approximately **four employees for two to three working days**, equivalent to about **64–96 direct employee-hours** at eight hours per day. The operational disruption can be more important than direct labour: routine warehouse activity is interrupted, while inventory errors may remain undiscovered between annual checks. Historical SAP count-line accuracy was reported at **97.36% in 2023**, **94.72% in 2024** and **91.41% in 2025**, with consistent definitions still to be confirmed.

## Proposed Solution

Introduce controlled ABC/risk-based counting integrated with approved SAP Business One data. Assign a manageable daily set of items/locations, provide appropriate localized count-area controls, record quantities, and route variances to authorized reviewers rather than posting unchecked adjustments. Current **provisional** intervals to evaluate are **A = 30, B = 90 and C = 365 calendar days**. These are **not a committed company policy** and should be calibrated against stock exposure, discrepancies, criticality, movement and practical count effort.

## Preliminary Analysis

A preliminary SAP study identified **3,833 stocked item records**; earlier partial classifications do not cover that entire universe and require reconciliation. Physical effort is not uniform. Concentrated engines and PTOs may be quick to verify together, bulky boxed components can sometimes be counted rapidly, and small fasteners may take longer to identify and count. A previous **15–20 minute daily physical-count** concept remains unvalidated, especially when preparation, travel, investigations and adjustments are considered.

## Expected Benefits

Earlier discrepancy detection, better inventory reliability and potentially less disruption from a multi-day annual count. The program could use **equal or greater total yearly labour** than the current count, so direct payroll savings are **not** assumed. Reduction or replacement of year-end counting must satisfy management, Finance and audit requirements.

## Pilot & Evaluation

Time representative A/B/C locations across item sizes and storage zones. Record preparation, movement controls, physical counting, variances, reviews, rechecks and effects on shipping/receiving. Validate item coverage, a workable calendar-to-workday schedule, accuracy and the actual total workload before setting final intervals.

## Primary Objective

Improve inventory accuracy and operational continuity with practical, controlled recurring checks, rather than merely reducing counted minutes.

Supporting evidence: [cycle-counting research](../../evidence/inventory-cycle-counting.md).

# Project 2 — Centralized Reporting & Automated Business Intelligence

## Current Challenge

Recurring departmental reporting can require employees to extract SAP data, assemble spreadsheets, reconcile values and distribute reports manually. Necessary oversight work can be time consuming and sometimes inconsistent across departments and branches.

## Proposed Solution

Pilot approved automated SAP data retrieval, standardized definitions and controlled scheduled reporting. Start by replicating one existing weekly service report and verifying its figures before automated distribution. Later, validated dashboards could support sales, inventory, purchasing, finance, service and shipping.

## Preliminary Analysis

The weekly service report has been **estimated** at **1–2 hours of manual preparation per week**, or **52–104 workload hours/year** over 52 weeks. This is neither a repeated timed baseline nor a confirmed automation saving. Other reports have not been consistently inventoried or measured.

## Expected Benefits

Potentially reduce recurring compilation and formatting, improve timely access and consistency, and give managers reliable information without repeated manual assembly. Review, exception management and maintenance remain necessary.

## Pilot & Evaluation

Document fields, data source, preparation steps, approvals and correction rates. Compare complete manual and assisted cycles, including validation and errors. Track development and support effort and annualize **net verified** handling time only. Confirm permissions and definitions with owners.

## Primary Objective

Improve reporting reliability and management visibility while reducing avoidable manual preparation, without weakening accuracy.

Supporting evidence: [reporting baseline](../../evidence/reporting-baseline.md).

# Project 3 — Automated Batch Shipping & SAP Integration

## Current Challenge

The existing workflow requires entering Purolator shipment details and printing a label, then locating the related order in SAP Business One, creating a delivery and printing its paperwork. Repeating the two-system sequence adds administrative work to each outgoing order. Prior handling measurements were approximately **30 seconds for Purolator** and **45 seconds for SAP**, or **75 seconds per shipment**.

## Proposed Solution

Build a controlled interface to enter or retrieve the sales-order number, package weight, dimensions and relevant carrier selections; then use approved integrations to prepare the Purolator label and corresponding SAP delivery. Batch processing may assist eligible orders, but serial numbers, mismatched lines, unusual shipments, voided labels and unsuccessful requests must prompt review. No uncontrolled SAP posting or assumed access is proposed.

## Preliminary Analysis

An additional historical review counted **2,020 completed shipments from October 2025 through September 2026**, excluding cancellations. The **proposed**, unverified assisted-handling target is **10–15 seconds** for an eligible shipment. Against 75 seconds, that implies **60–65 seconds potentially released per shipment**, a **80.0–86.7%** reduction. If every historical shipment were eligible and reached the target, the annual opportunity would be **33.7–36.5 employee-hours**. This is a **projection from measured and historical inputs**, not a realized trial result; some orders may not be suitable for automation.

## Expected Benefits

The direct dollar value of those hours might be relatively modest. The project remains useful as a clear demonstration of efficient workflow integration and a way to preserve shipping capacity during peak periods. Faster processing, fewer duplicate entries and greater consistency may have practical value, but time released does not imply fewer paid hours.

## Pilot & Evaluation

Time complete comparable manual and assisted shipments, including lookup, printing, SAP delivery, carrier acceptance, error recovery, retry/void cases and review. Verify order details, quantities, serials, labels, tracking and reconciliation. Establish actual eligible annual volume, operator time versus system wait time, integration costs, maintenance and exception rate.

## Primary Objective

Reduce repetitive shipping administration while keeping Purolator and SAP delivery processing accurate, supported and auditable.

Supporting evidence: [shipping calculations](../../evidence/shipping-baseline.md).

# Project 4 — Warehouse Slotting & Picking Optimization

## Current Challenge

Inventory locations in the Mississauga warehouse are influenced by product size, available storage and previous placement decisions. Some frequently picked items may be farther from the shipping and packing area than is operationally necessary. Because workers walk to retrieve products for multiple sales orders, even small unnecessary distances can add up over a working day. Any changes must preserve safe handling of large engines, PTOs, heavy parts and small components.

## Proposed Solution

Analyze **how often items are physically picked from warehouse locations** and recommend locating the most frequently picked products in the nearest suitable bins to shipping. Less frequently picked products could occupy more distant storage when size, capacity, handling equipment and accessibility allow. Use a warehouse distance map to compare present pick routes with proposed placements. The system would **recommend**, not automatically implement, inventory relocation.

## Preliminary Analysis

Approximately **five years of SAP sales, delivery, purchasing and inventory-movement history**, combined with bin-location records, can provide candidate evidence. However, sales revenue and total quantity shipped do **not** measure how many times a worker physically visits a bin. Direct shipments fulfilled by other branches, cancellations, returns, non-stock transactions and other nonlocal movements must be excluded or classified correctly. Actual physical-pick concentration should be calculated rather than assuming an 80/20 distribution.

Earlier exploratory warehouse work included a graph of nodes and travel edges and representative orders with different numbers of lines. That work demonstrates a possible simulation approach, **not a verified walking-distance reduction**. Reliable recommendations require validated locations, realistic route constraints and sample orders.

## Expected Benefits

Optimized placements may shorten average walking distance and pick-to-stage time, reduce fatigue and increase available picking capacity. These outcomes are **hypotheses**, not guaranteed percentages or financial savings. Any gain must be weighed against physical relocation effort, disrupted operations, storage capacity and potential congestion.

## Pilot & Evaluation

Build a verified current-location map. Calculate local physical-pick frequency from cleaned historical transactions. Simulate identical representative orders under current and proposed layouts, then seek approval for a limited, reversible relocation trial. Record walking distance, complete picking time, accuracy, relocation person-hours, access/safety constraints and operator feedback. Attribute placement-related walking gains here, **not again** to the RF Scanner pilot.

## Primary Objective

Use verified picking patterns to reduce avoidable warehouse travel while protecting safety, accuracy and practical stock accessibility.

Supporting evidence: [slotting analysis plan](../../evidence/warehouse-slotting.md).

# Project 5 — RF Scanner & Warehouse Management System

## Current Challenge

Warehouse receiving, picking and staging may involve repeated SAP screen navigation, order searches, location lookup and entry verification. These small tasks add friction during high-volume periods.

## Proposed Solution

Expand the **existing internally developed RF Scanner web application** into a controlled, mobile-friendly barcode interface for GRPO receiving, sales-order picking, item and bin lookup, staging/status and approved movement verification. SAP Business One remains the system of record.

## Preliminary Analysis

The implementation codebase exists at [jenozu/rf-scanner](https://github.com/jenozu/rf-scanner). Exact deployed modules, integration behaviour and readiness require code review and hands-on verification; an existing prototype is not proof of an end-to-end live workflow. Representative order sizes and receiving exceptions must be sampled.

## Expected Benefits

Possibly faster transaction lookup and confirmation, reduced keying errors and more dependable tracking of staged orders. These potential improvements should not be equated with automatic staffing reductions.

## Pilot & Evaluation

Compare complete manual and assisted receiving, lookup, picking and staging tasks—including scanning errors, serials, exceptions, correction, device costs and support. **Avoid double counting:** count verification belongs to Project 1, carrier/SAP shipping to Project 3, and location/walking improvements from stock relocation to Project 4.

## Primary Objective

Deliver a practical warehouse mobile interface that improves SAP transaction correctness and employee productivity while preserving authorized controls.

Supporting evidence: [RF Scanner evaluation](../../evidence/rf-scanner-pilot.md).
