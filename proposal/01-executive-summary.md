# 1. Executive Summary

Marindustrial has an opportunity to improve operational efficiency by developing internal software solutions tailored to the company’s existing workflows and business systems. Across warehouse, administrative, reporting, inventory, and shipping activities, many processes continue to involve repetitive data entry, document handling, movement between multiple systems, and recurring manual tasks. A dedicated internal software development capability could complement SAP Business One and other existing systems by simplifying these workflows, reducing unnecessary administrative effort, and allowing employees to focus more time on higher-value activities.

This proposal identifies five priority opportunities as practical starting points: automated inventory cycle counting; centralized reporting and business intelligence; automated batch shipping and SAP integration; automated direct-ship processing and SAP invoice management; and an expanded RF scanner and warehouse management system. Together, these projects demonstrate how targeted internal applications could support inventory accuracy, warehouse productivity, reporting visibility, shipping efficiency, and administrative processing. More importantly, they represent examples of a broader opportunity to continuously identify and address operational improvements across Marindustrial’s departments and branches.

Preliminary research indicates that several of these opportunities warrant further evaluation. Historical inventory counts have required approximately four employees over two to three working days. Current shipping administration has been measured at approximately 75 seconds per shipment, while a software-assisted process is expected to have the potential to reduce this to approximately 10–15 seconds. Existing prototypes and internal automation tools also provide preliminary evidence that selected workflows can be supported through custom software development. These findings represent measured conditions and early estimates rather than guaranteed savings or implementation results.

The recommended next step is therefore a controlled pilot phase. Selected processes can be tested using clearly defined existing and software-assisted workflows, allowing management to evaluate actual time reductions, operational improvements, accuracy, development requirements, maintenance needs, financial value, and implementation feasibility before considering broader deployment.

Management approval is requested to proceed with an initial pilot program for selected priority opportunities. If pilot results demonstrate sufficient and sustainable business value, Marindustrial could then evaluate establishing a dedicated internal software development role responsible for maintaining successful applications and continuously identifying new opportunities for operational improvement across the company.
