# 2. Opportunity Portfolio

The proposed internal software development capability is intended to improve processes throughout Marindustrial—not solely in shipping or the warehouse. The company's operations depend on employees, SAP Business One and complementary tools working together across departments and branches. Many routine tasks still require moving information between systems, preparing reports, reviewing documents, and verifying transaction details manually. Software tailored to these workflows could reduce repetitive effort while maintaining existing business controls.

| Area | Illustrative opportunities | Potential operational value |
|---|---|---|
| **Warehouse & Inventory Operations** | RF scanning, barcode-assisted receiving and picking, location lookup, staged orders, guided cycle counting | Faster transactions, more consistent verification, better inventory reliability |
| **Purchasing & Administration** | Purchase-order and invoice matching, supplier-document retrieval, automated attachment and validation, direct-ship processing | Less duplicate entry, faster document handling, fewer mismatches |
| **Reporting & Business Intelligence** | Scheduled service and departmental reports, dashboards for sales, inventory, purchasing, finance and shipping | Less time preparing recurring reports, improved consistency and management visibility |
| **Sales & Customer Service** | Order-status visibility, shipment notifications, product information retrieval, sales analytics | Faster responses and more dependable access to operational information |
| **Cross-Branch & Company-Wide Processes** | Standardized workflows across Mississauga, Montreal, Dartmouth and Moncton; shared integrations and common reporting definitions | Replicable improvements, reduced manual handoffs, more consistent practices |

The five priority pilot projects were selected as practical first examples of this portfolio. Some projects can stand alone; others support related processes and will need clear boundaries to avoid duplicate savings claims. The opportunity does not depend on building every conceivable tool, nor does it require replacing SAP. Successful applications would complement existing systems and be introduced using approved access, testing, employee review and operational safeguards.

A dedicated internal software developer could work with employees and management to identify friction points, document requirements, design and build targeted applications, test integration safely, support deployments, fix defects, and refine tools as business needs evolve. Familiarity with internal workflows may make iterative improvements easier to prioritize and maintain, but any internal-versus-external cost advantage must be established with comparable evidence.

**The long-term opportunity is a sustainable, cross-functional capability for continuous improvement.** The immediate opportunity is narrower: test selected ideas, measure the results and determine where future investment is justified.
