# Detailed Proposal

## Purpose
Develop a repeatable internal capability for finding and improving operational inefficiencies. Begin with tasks employees already identify as frustrating or repetitive. Measure the existing work before investing further.

## Working method
1. Ask employees which tasks consume time or regularly produce exceptions.
2. Document representative baseline measurements and the departments affected.
3. Select a small, testable improvement with clear success criteria.
4. Build or adapt the smallest solution and test it with users.
5. Measure before-and-after performance, corrections and ongoing costs.
6. Report both the positive results and any limitations.

## Proposed tool portfolio
See [[Ideas Index]]. Initial evaluation concentrates on the RF Scanner, invoice comparison/retrieval, landed-cost reconciliation, shipping and document preparation. The remaining ideas form a future backlog, not a promise to deliver all tools within 60 days.

## Working alongside existing systems
SAP Business One remains the system of record. Tools should use approved exports/imports or supported integrations. Human review and controls remain in place for financially significant or inventory-changing actions.

## Development economics
Internal development is potentially cost-effective because some prototypes exist and staff understand the workflows. Compare *remaining* internal investment with genuinely comparable vendor quotes, taking into account employee time, testing, maintenance, support, security, integration and continuity.

## Commercialization
Build for our own operation first. After proven value, ask whether other organizations have the same problem, what must be generalized, who owns the resulting intellectual property, and whether licensing/support is commercially viable. See [[Commercialization Strategy]].

## Financial standard
Every tool has one summary and one detailed CBA with separate **internal annual value** and **illustrative external commercial potential**. Capacity released is not automatically payroll savings. Never use hypothetical sales to justify internal payback.

## Pilot and decision
A 60-day pilot validates a limited set of workflows and replaces estimates with observed measurements. Management receives evidence, costs, open risks and a possible next-stage roadmap. See [[60-Day Pilot Plan]] and [[Cost-Benefit Methodology]].
