# Cost-Benefit Methodology

## Short management view: the five questions
1. What costs or delays exist now?
2. What changes for staff?
3. What annual measurable internal value is expected?
4. What additional investment and ongoing support are required?
5. Could the tool be offered externally, and what evidence supports that idea?

## Internal annual-value calculation
`Annual hours released = (baseline minutes − after minutes) × annual transactions / 60`

`Annual usable capacity value = hours released × loaded hourly cost × demonstrated usable-capacity factor`

`Net annual operational value = annual usable capacity value + verified other incremental benefits − annual incremental recurring costs`

Show actual cash savings **separately** from released capacity. Annualizing past or sample observations requires a credible representative volume and seasonality assumption.

`Simple payback in months = remaining one-time investment ÷ (annual net operational value / 12)` only when the net value is positive and steady-state; explain benefit ramp-up.

## External commercial model
`Recurring gross revenue = paying customers × monthly price × 12`; add separately reported one-time setup revenue. **Commercial contribution = revenue − ongoing costs of serving customers**, which may be negative. Development/generalization investment and customer acquisition belong in the external model. Do not combine hypothetical external revenue with the internal payback.

## Confidence labels
**Measured** (actual observed tests); **Calculated** (transparent formula using documented inputs); **Estimated** (reasonable but unverified); **Scenario** (illustrative hypothetical customers/prices); **Unknown** (do not invent a number).

## Avoid double-counting
RF receiving, wave picking and inventory counting are part of the RF business case unless explicitly carved out. Likewise, customer notifications and the customer portal may reduce the same inquiry. Assign one owner to each benefit before generating a portfolio total.

## External-vendor comparison
Require like-for-like written scope and quotes. Compare remaining internal investment, external development/setup, licences, integrations, security, staff training, support, maintenance and continuity, over a declared time horizon.
