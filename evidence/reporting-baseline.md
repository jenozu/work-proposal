# Reporting and BI — baseline assumptions

## Identified opportunity

A weekly service report is estimated to require **1–2 hours/week** to prepare. Assuming 52 occurrences/year, this equals **52–104 hours/year of current manual workload**. It is **not** 52–104 guaranteed hours saved: automated report production may still require data-quality checks, reviews, exceptions, modifications and maintenance.

Other functions may have recurring reports—inventory, purchasing, sales, finance, shipping and multi-branch management—but their exact number, frequency and effort have not been measured consistently.

## Pilot to establish a baseline

Choose the service report as a repeatable first case. For at least several typical cycles, record:

| Item | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Report frequency | Weekly (initial case) | Same deliverable |
| Source access and extraction | Time to measure | Time to measure |
| Data cleaning and formatting | Time to measure | Time to measure |
| Verification and approval | Time to measure | Time to measure |
| Corrections / exceptions | Count and time | Count and time |
| Output accuracy and freshness | Confirm definitions | Confirm definitions |
| Development and ongoing support | N/A for baseline | Estimate and record |

The published management summary should annualize **net saved minutes per report × actual number of reports**, subject to user adoption and exception rates. Do not extrapolate this one example to all departments without additional evidence.

Source: October 2026 preliminary service-report discussion.
